# Little Works — grow wisely

Five experiments, one question at a time:
1. Shared costs: one fixed-capacity workshop, £100 fixed/day plus £4 per toy; run 10 and 20 toys. Total cost rises while average falls (£14 → £9). Call this better utilization of an existing workshop, a precursor to long-run scale reasoning.
2. Bottleneck: confirmed order 80, assembly capacity 80, packing capacity 40. Assembly upgrade costs another £100/day and doubles assembly; packing upgrade costs £40/day and doubles packing. They are mutually exclusive experiment configurations. Compare completed output, unfilled orders and costs. Only the packing upgrade fulfills 80 (£6.50/toy).
3. Expansion: a new disclosed daily model permits 1–6 teams, 20 toys/team. Fixed = £100 + £25(n−1); coordination = £15(n−1)²; variable = £4 per completed toy. Confirmed demand 80. Choose the cheapest plan that fulfills all orders (4 teams, £7.875/toy displayed £7.88). Smaller plans can look cheaper per unit while leaving orders unfilled. Compare all six plans, explicitly marking infeasible ones. Economies/diseconomies are tested by comparing full-capacity average costs, not just fuller use of an unchanged plant.
4. Demand shock: same expansion model, confirmed demand falls to 20. Four teams now cost £19.50/toy; one costs £9. Customers buy only the confirmed orders, production stops at demand, and idle capacity still incurs fixed and coordination costs.
5. Transfer: a food festival with 40 confirmed customers and £15/meal. One stall: £120 fixed + £3/meal, capacity40, £360 profit. Three-stall operation: £300 fixed + £180 coordination + £2/meal, capacity120, £40 profit. Choose a plan and justify the profit comparison. Novel figures and a different context test a bounded response, not broad mastery.

Rules: one representative toy in the scene is marked as an illustration, not counted output; numeric output/queues/capacities are model values. Finished output = min(orders, assembly, packing). All output is sold at £15 and meets the same stipulated quality; variable costs occur only for completed units. Fixed charges are daily rental/service costs for each prospective configuration, not irreversible past capital purchases.
